What we are tracking

One month in, AI assistants recommend more of your pages than they did in July. And one page shows exactly where your next opportunity is.

There are now two different doors into your shop, and they no longer open together.

For years, being findable meant one thing: showing up high in Google’s list of blue links. That still matters, and plenty of your customers shop exactly that way.

But a growing number never see that list. They ask an assistant a question in plain English and get back one answer, with a handful of shops named in it. Getting named in that answer is a different thing from showing up high in Google’s list, and the two have come apart. Being strong in one does not carry you in the other.

That is why this page has two sections instead of one. The first asks: do assistants name you? The second asks: where do you sit in Google’s list? A page can be doing well in one and invisible in the other, and one of yours is exactly that. Neither door replaces the other. The goal is to have both of them open.

Four words we use a lot, in plain terms. Being named is when an assistant mentions your shop in its answer. A listing is one time your page showed up in somebody’s Google results. Your spot is how far down that Google list you were, where 1st to 10th is the first page and almost nobody scrolls past it. A click from Google is somebody seeing your listing and choosing to open it.

The big one: one door is wide open and the other is shut, on the same page. Assistants name your Wave Stacking Rings every single time we ask. Google has that same page sitting 29th, where it was shown 241 times last month and got not one visit. Same page, same month. Shoppers who ask an assistant are already finding that page. Shoppers who type into Google effectively cannot.

Mountain is your strongest theme, and it is holding. 12th place on “silver mountain jewelry”, with real searching behind it. That is the top of page two, one good push from page one.

Ocean is your biggest opening. People are searching for ocean jewelry about thirty different ways, and your collection showed up 421 times this month. Almost all of those were past 45th place, so nobody sees it. The wanting is proven. The page is not yet strong enough to meet it.

Two blog posts are carrying your search traffic: the one on keeping a necklace clasp in back, and the one on adjustable rings. Between them they were seen 2,984 times, more than the whole rest of your site put together. That makes them the most valuable place you have to point a reader next.
